The Essay

We named it after the thing we were losing.

How Kizzuna started, what we're building with it, and exactly where the money goes.
By the Kizzuna team

Kizzuna comes from the Japanese 絆, kizuna. It means the bond between people, the kind that only holds if somebody keeps tending it. We picked the name before we had a film, a budget, or much of a plan, because it named the thing we thought was quietly going missing.

It started as a two-week digital detox in high school. A few of us put the phones in a drawer, and the two weeks turned into a question we couldn't drop, which turned into a camera, which turned into a film shot across the country. Somewhere in there it stopped being a project and became the thing we do.

What we're building is not complicated. A documentary called Disconnected, made by people who grew up inside the problem rather than watching it from across a generation. And a program that carries it into schools, so the film starts a conversation instead of ending one.

That second half is the entire point.

A film that screens once, gets a round of applause, and disappears has done nothing. •

We want the assembly, then the classroom session the week after, then the parent evening where somebody finally says the quiet thing out loud. That is the part that moves anything, and it is the part nobody funds.

None of it has run on our own pockets alone. Here's the backing we've earned so far.

What we've won so far Raised to date · $46,000
LookUp Innovation Challenge$2,500
UROP Film Grant$6,000

Plus recognition from the Savitt Scholars and the Allstate Foundation College Service Grant.

The film starts going into classrooms this fall. That is what the money is really for.

Nobody at Kizzuna takes a salary. That is not a virtue, it is just the math of three people doing this around school and work.

We're an LLC, not a registered nonprofit, so giving us money is a gift and not a tax write-off. We say that in every issue because it's true, and because you should know exactly what you're doing when you click the button. We're working on the fiscal sponsorship that would change it.

The goal this year is simple: Finish the film, and get it in front of our first ten to fifteen schools, on the way to a much bigger number we're not shy about. Four hundred.

Thanks for being here this early.

Ben, Kei & Valentin
